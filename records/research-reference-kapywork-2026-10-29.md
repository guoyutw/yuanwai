## Potentially reusable observations (from the external report)

- KapyWork presents a low-friction job-posting proposition for employers: free posting, browser access without requiring an app, and contact/application features. **Reported platform proposition, not verified user acquisition.**
- The external report cites a founder/official announcement of 200+ cumulative job posts and a website display of 100+ currently listed jobs around October 2026. **Different measures may coexist**; neither demonstrates successful placements, retention or revenue.
- The report identifies an official announcement about suspending a suspicious listing/account after a user report. **Moderation action was reported; staffing, review process and automation level remain unknown.**
- The founder's pre-existing content audience and food-industry connections **could** help initial supplier acquisition. No attributable acquisition channel, conversion rate or causal effect was established.

## Comparison question for Yuanwai (INFERENCE, not requirement)

Both concepts connect a demand side to a supply side, but KapyWork's job listings appear comparatively standardized; Yuanwai's catering/group-meal requests involve conditional questions, supplier feasibility, approval and real-world fulfillment. The useful comparison is therefore **how a small platform recruits and serves both sides while minimizing routine human coordination**, not which website UI to reproduce.

When Yuanwai has legitimate real-case evidence, useful questions to revisit are:
1. Where did the first reliable suppliers and customers actually come from, and at what acquisition effort?
2. Which steps were genuinely self-service, which required human intervention, and what did those interventions cost?
3. What proved a meaningful match or completed service, rather than merely a listing, inquiry or message?
4. Which repeatable low-risk coordination steps could an AI steward handle, and which commercial/fulfillment promises still require specific human approval?
